# Weekly Review & Weekly Habits / Routines Framework

## Purpose

Create a simple, repeatable system that connects longer-term goals to the habits and routines carried out each week. The system should make it easy to decide what matters, follow through during the week, and learn from what actually happened without creating a large administrative burden.

## Principles

- Start small: use the minimum structure needed to make the next week clearer.
- Keep one source of truth for current goals, weekly commitments, habits, and routines.
- Prefer repeatable checklists and templates over relying on memory.
- Review outcomes without judgement; missed actions are information for improving the system.
- Add complexity only when a recurring problem shows that it is needed.

## How the system works

The framework runs as a weekly loop:

1. **Review** the previous week.
2. **Choose** the few outcomes that matter most for the coming week.
3. **Plan** the habits, routines, and one-off actions that support those outcomes.
4. **Execute** from a lightweight daily view.
5. **Track** enough information to support the next review.

The weekly review is the control point. Goals provide direction, the weekly plan narrows that direction into realistic commitments, and habits and routines make recurring work easier to carry out.

## Elements

### Goals

Maintain a short list of active goals. Each goal should state the desired outcome, why it matters, and the next meaningful milestone. During the weekly review, confirm that each planned action or recurring behaviour supports an active goal or a necessary area of life.

### Weekly review

Use the same checklist each week:

1. Collect loose notes, tasks, and commitments.
2. Review the calendar, completed work, habit tracking, and unfinished items.
3. Record wins, friction, and lessons from the week.
4. Check progress on active goals.
5. Decide what to continue, change, defer, or stop.
6. Select up to three priority outcomes for the next week.
7. Schedule important actions and confirm the week's habits and routines.

The review should normally fit within 30–45 minutes. If it repeatedly takes longer, reduce the number of items being reviewed before adding automation.

### Weekly plan

The weekly plan contains:

- up to three priority outcomes;
- the next actions required for each outcome;
- fixed calendar commitments;
- habits and routines planned for the week;
- a short "not this week" list to make trade-offs explicit.

Unfinished work should not roll forward automatically. Recommit to it, rescope it, schedule it later, or remove it.

### Habits

Habits are small behaviours to repeat consistently, such as exercising or writing. Each habit should define:

- the behaviour;
- its trigger or intended time;
- a minimum viable version for difficult days;
- the target frequency;
- a simple done/not-done measure.

Start with no more than three habits. Review trends weekly rather than trying to maintain a perfect streak.

### Routines

Routines are short checklists for recurring sequences, such as a morning setup, end-of-day shutdown, or the weekly review itself. Each routine should have a clear trigger and completion point. Keep routine steps in order and remove any step that does not consistently add value.

### Daily use

Daily planning should take only a few minutes:

1. Check the calendar and weekly priorities.
2. Choose the most important next action for the day.
3. Follow the relevant routines and record completed habits.
4. Capture new tasks or notes for later processing instead of reorganising the whole system.

## Minimum viable version

The first version needs only three documents:

1. **Active goals:** goal, reason, milestone, and next action.
2. **Weekly review and plan:** the review checklist, reflections, priorities, actions, and "not this week" list.
3. **Habits and routines:** current habit definitions, a weekly done/not-done tracker, and routine checklists.

Run this version for four weeks before expanding it. The first iteration can use plain documents and a calendar; a dashboard, integrations, reminders, or automation are not required.

## Rollout

### Phase 1: Establish the loop

- Create the three minimum documents.
- Pick a regular weekly review time.
- Select one to three habits and only the routines already needed.
- Complete four weekly cycles and note points of friction.

### Phase 2: Refine

- Simplify templates based on actual use.
- Add a monthly goal check if weekly reviews are too tactical.
- Add reminders or calendar blocks where missed triggers are a recurring problem.
- Archive previous weekly plans so progress and patterns remain visible.

### Phase 3: Automate selectively

Consider a dashboard or integrations only for repetitive work that has persisted through the first two phases, such as preparing the review, showing habit trends, or carrying calendar data into the weekly plan.

## Measures of success

After four weeks, the framework is working if:

- weekly reviews happen consistently and produce a clear plan;
- each week has no more than three explicit priority outcomes;
- active goals have visible next actions;
- habits and routines are easy to complete or intentionally adjust;
- fewer commitments are forgotten or carried forward without a decision; and
- maintaining the system feels lighter than the confusion it removes.

The main measure is not the amount of data collected. It is whether the system makes the next useful action easier to see and repeat.
